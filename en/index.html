2. Principle

PersOS works fully without an account. Everything you enter is stored on your device only by default. There is no tracking, no analytics, no advertising, and no sharing with third parties for advertising or analytics. Data leaves your device only if you deliberately use or turn on one of the features described below.

3. Data on your device

The app stores what you enter locally, for example:

  • Profile (name, preferred form of address, age statement (age stage and release date, no date of birth), life stage, active areas)
  • Tasks, inbox, day planning, notes, goals, focus sessions
  • Study and work (timetable, assignments, exams, grades, flashcards, formulas, job applications, work log and performance review)
  • Workouts, training plans, meals, recipes, shopping list, water, weight

This data lives in the app's database on your device. We have no access to it. It is deleted when you delete the app.

Device only, and not in sync or backups either: conversations with the Companion (the app's AI assistant), job suggestions from saved searches and replies to job applications that you paste or share. Such a reply only waits until you sort it; then its text is discarded, and only the new status, an appointment or your own short note is kept.

Applying by email: The app prepares the email; it is sent through iOS's Mail app with your own account. PersOS does not read your mailbox. After sending, the app keeps only the recipient and the subject, not the text.

Crash reports: When the app crashes or hangs, iOS provides a technical report (stack trace, device model, iOS version, no content from the app). PersOS keeps the last ten on the device, not in sync and not in backups. Nothing is sent automatically; under Settings › Data & Backup you can share or delete the reports.

Sketches in notes: Drawings you make on a note, and the text the app recognises in them (on the device only), are kept on your device and in your encrypted backup. They are not synced (neither with an account nor through iCloud) and never sent to any AI.

Search by meaning and translation: Search by meaning (related entries, note search, choosing what the Companion reads) is computed on the device with a system language model; for this the app keeps a search index on the device: all your entries in short passages (for example also your journal, meal plan and Companion conversations) and the computed meaning values (vectors). The index file and the vectors live in their own file on the device, are rebuilt from your data, are not in backups or exports, are not synced and never leave the device. What the Companion reads from the index follows the areas you have allowed (section 7). Entries from Fitness & Health, including the journal and meals, never go to the cloud AI; only the Companion on the device reads them. Companion conversations are searchable but are never given to the Companion as context. Deleting the app removes the index with it. Translations (flashcards, notes, Companion answers) are done by iOS's translation sheet on the device; the system downloads the language models from Apple once, without an account, and your texts do not leave the device for it.

4. Apple frameworks

iOS asks for each of the following permissions separately. You can revoke them at any time in iOS Settings.

  • Apple Health: The app reads health values (e.g. HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and sleep stages, activity, workouts, weight, VO2 max, SpO2, respiratory rate, blood pressure, blood glucose, body composition, nutrition) to display them and to add workouts to your log. It writes strength workouts, meals, water if you choose, and a weight you enter in the app to Apple Health. Health values from Apple Health never leave your device: they are not synced, not sent to any AI and not transmitted to us.
  • Calendar and Reminders: to show and create events and reminders, to read a timetable from a (possibly subscribed) calendar, and to add your timetable to a calendar of its own. The data stays in your calendar and reminders.
  • Camera: to scan documents and exams, capture barcodes on food, and photograph meals. Analysis happens on the device unless you explicitly choose cloud analysis (see section 7).
  • Microphone (dictation): only while you have tapped the microphone button and are dictating. What you say is turned into text on the device; nothing is recorded, stored or sent. The system downloads the language model for your language from Apple once, without an account.
  • Music: to show the current track during a workout and control playback.
  • Apple Watch: workouts and routines can be sent to your Apple Watch via WorkoutKit.
  • Siri, Shortcuts, share menu, widgets and notifications: run locally on your device. Notifications are scheduled on the device, not sent through a server.

The app does not ask for your location.

5. Online lookups you request

Some features query public services, only when you use them. Only what the lookup needs is transmitted. For technical reasons the service also receives your IP address. No account is needed, and we receive no data in the process.

  • Open Food Facts (Open Food Facts, France): when you scan a barcode or tap "Search online". Only the barcode or your search words are sent. Never while typing, never in the background.
  • USDA FoodData Central (US Department of Agriculture, USA): when you tap "General foods (USDA)". Your search term goes through our server (Supabase, EU) to USDA; our server does not store or log it. A non-English search term is translated into English on your device first (iOS translation; the system downloads the language models from Apple once). Never while typing, never in the background.
  • Recipe link: when you import a recipe from a link, your device loads exactly that one page directly from the address you entered (HTTPS only) and reads the recipe on the device. As with any browser visit, the site sees your IP address; nothing passes through our server.
  • Nutrition table: A photo or scan of a nutrition table is read on the device and discarded afterwards.
  • Calendar and notice links (university portals): If you paste a calendar link (iCal) or an RSS link from your portal, your device fetches exactly that address directly (HTTPS only) when you tap "Fetch" and, once you have reviewed the first deadlines, at launch and when returning to the app (at most hourly). The link often holds a secret key of your portal; it is stored only in this device's Keychain, not in sync, not in backups and not in the export, and is never logged. As with any request the portal sees your IP address; nothing passes through our server. Deleting the link in the app also removes it from the Keychain.
  • Job posting from a link: When you import a job from a link, your device loads exactly that one page directly from the address you entered (HTTPS only) and reads the posting on the device. The site sees your IP address; nothing passes through our server.
  • Job search of the German Federal Employment Agency (Bundesagentur für Arbeit): when you search for jobs or save a search. Your search terms, place, radius and filters are sent. The app occasionally re-runs saved searches in the background to suggest new postings; only these search details are sent then as well.
  • Apple Maps: when you look up a place for a job application. Only what you type into the search field is sent to Apple.
  • Apple Weather and Apple Maps (the "Unterwegs" module): only if you turn the module on and set a home place. When you open the app it then asks Apple Weather for the weather at the coordinates of your home place and of the place of your current or next appointment, asks Apple Maps for the travel time to your appointments (from your home place or from the place of the appointment right before) and looks up places named in your appointments (the place text is sent to Apple). Once a week it loads the Apple Weather mark for the attribution. No account is needed. Your current location is never requested. The places you set are stored in your profile (locally, in backups, and in sync only if you turned it on). Weather and travel times are cached on the device only, not in sync and not in backups.

The privacy policies of these services apply.

6. Optional sync

In Settings you can choose one of two sync targets, or leave sync off (default).

End-to-end encrypted: Before data leaves your device for sync, it is encrypted on the device (AES-256-GCM). Only your devices have the key: it is kept in your iCloud Keychain and, encrypted with your recovery code, as a backup on the server. Neither we nor Supabase nor Apple can read the content. Only technical details that sync needs stay unencrypted: a random identifier per entry, the time of its last change and, if applicable, deletion, plus an opaque, keyed integrity value used to verify rows and resolve conflicts with identical timestamps. We never store the recovery code; if it is lost and no device with the key is left, the cloud data can no longer be read. Requests to the cloud AI (section 7) are not end-to-end encrypted: they are a deliberate choice per conversation.

6.1 Account (Supabase)

  • Sign-in: with Sign in with Apple or Google. We receive a user ID and the email address that Apple or Google provides (with Apple possibly an anonymous relay address). The privacy policies of Apple or Google also apply to the sign-in itself.
  • Synced data: the app content listed in section 3, so it is the same on all your devices. Excluded: heart rate and energy values of workouts, all values from Apple Health and WHOOP, Companion conversations and job suggestions.
  • Provider: Supabase Inc. as processor. The database is hosted in a data center in the EU. As Supabase Inc. is a US company, access from the US cannot be fully ruled out. This is covered by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses.
  • Signing out stops sync. Local data is kept.

6.2 iCloud

Data is stored in your private iCloud database at Apple. It is only accessible with your Apple ID. We cannot read it. Apple's privacy policy applies. The same exclusions apply as for the account.

6.3 Sharing

You can send individual items (e.g. a deck of cards, a recipe or a routine) to others as a file or link. Nothing is sent to us. Only the content itself is shared, never your study progress, grades or health values.

With a link, the content sits in the part after the `#`. Browsers never send this part to a server. The info page for recipients without the app is hosted on GitHub Pages; when the link is opened, GitHub technically sees the IP address, but never the content. Anyone with the link can read the content – so only share it with people you want to give it to.

7. AI features

  • Default: AI features run on your device (Apple Foundation Models). No data leaves the device.
  • Apple's server (optional, iOS 27 and later): Under Apple Intelligence you can allow larger tasks to use Apple's server (Private Cloud Compute). The switch is off by default, applies to this device only and is not part of backups; the app explains what is sent when you turn it on. It needs no account with us, and requests do not go through our server. What is sent is your message and the entries from the areas the Companion may read, including health values. According to Apple, Apple does not store the content; processing takes place in data centres of Apple and Google Cloud under the same guarantees. There is a daily limit across your iCloud account. Below every answer you can see which model answered. Without a connection, at the limit or during an outage, the task keeps running on the device.
  • Cloud AI (optional): only with an account and after you turn it on in Settings. The switch applies to this device only and is not part of backups.
  • Providers: Cloud AI is available from age 18 (section 13). Requests go through our server (Supabase, EU) to the provider you choose in Settings: Mistral AI SAS (France, processed in data centres in EU and EFTA countries), Anthropic PBC (USA, model "Claude") or OpenAI (USA, model "ChatGPT"). With Anthropic and OpenAI, your data is processed in a third country.
  • What is sent: Before the first message of a conversation, the app shows which areas the Companion may read. Below every answer you can see which entries were actually sent. Photos and documents (e.g. a meal photo, a timetable as PDF or photo, a scanned exam) are only sent when you explicitly choose cloud analysis for them.
  • We neither store nor log request content. We only store a count of requests per day and account to limit abuse.
  • Health values are never sent to the cloud AI.
  • Better recognition (text recognition): For lecture material or an imported document you can choose "Besser erkennen". The pages you confirm for that document then go through our server to Mistral AI SAS (processed in the EU), even if you chose another provider for the Companion. Only the recognised text comes back. We neither store nor log content, only a count of pages per day and account. The same age limit of 18 applies.
  • Job applications: "Draft with AI" sends, in the cloud, the company, the position, the application's notes, the contact person, your name and your template; the app asks before the first time, and below every draft it shows what was sent. When sorting a reply, the app shows the email text that would be sent before every cloud request and sends it only after you confirm. The email text comes from a third party; the AI may only suggest a kind from it, and only what you tap is applied.
  • Changes the AI suggests are either applied only after you confirm them, or applied at once with a visible receipt and an undo option.

8. WHOOP (optional)

If you connect your WHOOP account, you sign in directly with WHOOP (OAuth). The app then fetches recovery, strain and sleep values from the WHOOP API and displays them.

  • Access tokens are stored only in your device's Keychain, not in sync and not in backups.
  • To exchange and refresh tokens, our server (Supabase, EU) forwards the request to WHOOP. Nothing is stored or logged in the process.
  • WHOOP values stay on the device like all health values.
  • "Disconnect" deletes the tokens. WHOOP, Inc.'s privacy policy applies to data held by WHOOP.

Strava (optional)

If you connect your Strava account, you sign in directly with Strava (OAuth). The app then loads your activities from the last 30 days (time, sport, distance, average heart rate) and adds them to the log as workouts; an identical workout from Apple Health or WHOOP is completed rather than duplicated.

  • Access tokens are stored only in your device's Keychain, not in sync and not in backups.
  • To exchange and refresh tokens, our server (Supabase, EU) forwards the request to Strava. Nothing is stored or logged in the process; your activities never pass through our server.
  • Imported workouts are ordinary workouts of the log, with its rules for sync, backup and export (sections 6 and 9).
  • "Disconnect" deletes the tokens and revokes access at Strava; workouts already imported stay in the log. Strava, Inc.'s privacy policy applies to data held by Strava.

Mood and mindfulness from Apple Health

With your permission the app reads mood entries (from Health, the Journal app or the Watch) and mindful minutes from Apple Health and shows them as a trend. They stay on the device like all health values and never go to the cloud.

Body state: recovery, load and sleep

From Apple Health (HRV, heart rate during sleep, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, wrist temperature, sleep, active energy, steps, workout effort) and your training log, the app computes your recovery, load, sleep need and sleep debt on the device. For the maximum heart rate it reads your date of birth and sex from Apple Health, for the calculation only; neither is stored. The computed daily values and your stated sleep need stay on the device and in the backup (section 9), but are never synced and never sent to a cloud AI. Your rating of how hard a workout was is synced like the other workout details.

9. Export and backup

You can export all your data to a file and store it wherever you like. The file is encrypted with your passphrase. We have neither the file nor the passphrase. A lost passphrase cannot be recovered. The export contains no sign-in tokens.

11. Retention and deletion

  • Local data: until you delete it or remove the app.
  • Account and sync data: until the account is deleted. You can delete it yourself at any time under Profile and Settings → your profile card → Delete Account. All associated server data is deleted immediately. If you signed in with Apple, we also revoke the link to your Apple ID. You decide whether the data on the device is kept or deleted as well.
  • iCloud data: managed through your iCloud settings.
  • AI request counter: deleted with the account.
  • Age marker in the keychain: It restricts only until the release date or until Apple reports "18 or older". The entry itself stays on the device: it stays with "Delete Account, Keep Data Here" and with "Delete Account and Data on This Device" (section 13), and iOS decides whether it survives deleting the app. Resetting the device removes it.
  • Flag on the account: deleted with the account.

13. Children

PersOS is also meant for school students. This is why the app knows your age in stages (under 13, 13 to 15, 16 or 17, 18 and over) and protects minors further. Without the optional cloud features (account, WHOOP, Strava, sharing), only the lookups in section 5 leave the device, and only when you trigger them.

Where the app gets your age: During setup the app first asks Apple for the age range of your Apple account, if you allow it. Apple tells the app only an age stage and the kind of statement (made by you, set by your parents in Family Sharing, or confirmed), no date of birth. If you do not share it or there is none, you state your age yourself: first "Are you 18 or older?", and if not, the month and year in which you turn 18. From that the app works out your stage and the release date (the 1st of the month after the month in which you turn 18). Only this date is stored, no date of birth. The date does allow your month and year of birth to be inferred, however; if you are signed in, it is also kept on the server with your account (see below). We do not check any ID. If Apple's statement and your own differ, the stricter one applies.

What is possible at which stage:

  • Under 13: There is no account, no sharing, no connection to WHOOP or Strava and no Apple server model. Your data stays on the device; only sync through your own iCloud works, if you turn it on. If you say you are under 13, the app ends an existing sign-in and disconnects WHOOP and Strava; your data on the device stays.
  • 13 to 15: Account, WHOOP, Strava, sharing and Apple's server only work if you confirm in the app that your parents agree, separately for the account (with WHOOP and Strava), for sharing and for Apple's server. Only the time of the confirmation is stored, and that you gave it yourself. All of these are your own statements. Under "Schutz & Alter" (Protection & Age) you can see the confirmations and withdraw them at any time; for the account, the app then ends the sign-in and disconnects WHOOP and Strava.
  • 16 and 17: Account, WHOOP, Strava, sharing and Apple's server work without a confirmation.
  • Cloud AI is available from age 18. For minors it is off with every provider and cannot be turned on. AI on the device and sync through iCloud are open to everyone.

If you have not answered the question yet (for example after an update), the strict limits for minors apply for now: cloud AI is off, counting food and body weight are hidden, and the Companion asks before writing and speaks simply. The quiet evenings below apply only once you have stated your age. An existing sign-in stays until you answer, new connections are blocked, and your data on the device is not touched.

Further limits for everyone under 18:

  • Counting food (calories, nutrition values, nutrition goals) and body weight are hidden. Existing entries are not deleted.
  • Once you have stated that you are a minor, the app does not start notifications on its own between 9 pm and 7 am. Some arrive at 7 am instead, others are dropped. What you set up yourself (for example reminders) stays.
  • The Companion writes only after you confirm.
  • The Companion on the device is instructed to speak simply, to give no advice on diets, calories or weight, and to point you to a trusted adult and to the helpline Nummer gegen Kummer (116 111) if you are not feeling well. This is an instruction to a language model and not a guarantee: it may not follow it. What you can rely on are the hidden features (counting food, body weight) and the confirmation before writing. If the device model declines a message, the app shows the pointer to a trusted adult and 116 111.

What is stored where:

  • In the profile are the age stage, its source (Apple or stated by you), the release date, the time from which the statement is binding, and the times of your parents' confirmations. They belong to your profile: they are stored on your device and, like the rest of the profile, included in sync (section 6) and export (section 9).
  • A marker in your device's keychain is created as soon as you are a minor. It holds the age stage from Apple (if the statement came from Apple), the release date and the time of the marker. It serves the protection of minors: the statement should not be loosened again by a reinstall, a new account or another device with sync. The marker applies to this device only, is not in the iCloud keychain, not in backups and not in sync, and never reaches us. It only ever becomes stricter, never looser. It stays even if you choose "Delete Account, Keep Data Here" or "Delete Account and Data on This Device" under "Konto & Synchronisierung" (Account & Sync). Whether it survives deleting the app is up to iOS. It restricts only until the release date or, if it came from Apple, as soon as Apple reports "18 or older"; for this the app asks Apple at most once a day.
  • On the account we store, if you are signed in and a minor, two details: whether Apple reported "minor" (yes or no) and the release date, plus the time of the last change. No date of birth, no stage. They are set through protected server functions that can only tighten existing restrictions. They make our cloud AI function refuse requests from minors, also after a reinstall or on another device. If the server cannot read the details, it refuses the request. It compares the date with the time in the westernmost time zone (UTC−12), so it never releases early anywhere, but in some time zones up to about a day later than your device. The details are deleted with the account.

After setup: You can correct a statement freely only until the end of setup. After that your age in the app changes only through Apple's age statement or on the release date; the app no longer accepts a statement of your own. Sync and importing an export accept only stricter statements and never loosen the statement in the app. If you entered yourself as a minor by mistake, the limits apply until the release date.

Shared devices: The marker applies to the device. If you share a device, the limits apply to everyone until the release date.

The gap: This is protection on trust, not an age check. Anyone who states a wrong age at the very first start is not detected without an age statement from Apple (for example from Family Sharing). The server does not check age either: a report from the app cannot remove an existing Apple minor flag on the account. A server-verified release process is not currently available, so previously flagged adults may temporarily remain restricted. If you reset the device, the marker is gone; with the same account the flag on the account remains.
